> “Our ads bring bad leads.”

That conclusion can feel obvious when many enquiries stop replying, ask only about price, or never book a trial. But the ad platform is only one part of the system that turns interest into a sales conversation.

Before changing the targeting or creative, check what happens after someone submits a form or sends a message. Slow responses, unclear questions, and weak handoffs can make interested prospects look unqualified.

## Start by defining what a lead becomes

Teams often use the word “lead” for several different stages. That makes it difficult to see where quality drops.

A practical lead journey might include:

- **New enquiry:** someone submits a form or starts a WhatsApp conversation.
- **Contacted lead:** the team has sent a useful first response.
- **Responsive lead:** the prospect replies and continues the conversation.
- **Qualified lead:** the prospect fits the service, location, budget, or learning need.
- **Trial booked:** a specific trial date and time have been agreed.
- **Trial attended:** the prospect actually joins the trial session.

Each stage answers a different question. If you measure only cost per enquiry, you cannot tell whether the ads, the offer, or the follow-up process needs attention.

## Response time changes the evidence

A person who enquires after seeing an ad may be comparing several tutoring centres or language courses. Their intent is often strongest in the first few minutes.

If the first useful reply arrives hours later, the prospect may already be speaking with another provider. When they stop replying, the lead is recorded as bad even though the original interest was real.

Track the time between the enquiry and the first meaningful response. An automated greeting is helpful, but it is not the same as answering the question or moving the conversation forward.

The goal is not constant pressure. It is a fast, relevant response that helps the prospect decide on the next step.

## Check the four places where good leads get lost

### 1. The ad sets the wrong expectation

The message may attract attention without explaining the programme, location, age group, price range, or intended outcome. A clearer offer can reduce curiosity clicks and prepare prospects for a useful conversation.

### 2. The first reply creates friction

Long scripts and too many questions can make the conversation feel like a form. Start with the information the prospect needs, then ask one or two questions that help determine fit.

### 3. Qualification is inconsistent

If every customer service representative uses a different definition of a qualified lead, campaign feedback becomes unreliable. Agree on a small set of criteria and record them consistently.

### 4. The path to a trial is vague

“Would you like more information?” leaves the next step open. A concrete invitation with available trial times makes it easier for an interested prospect to act.

## Use a simple follow-up checklist

### Before the campaign

- Define who the programme is for.
- Decide what makes a lead qualified.
- Prepare answers for the most common questions.
- Make trial availability clear to the team.

### When a new enquiry arrives

- Respond while the interest is still fresh.
- Answer the prospect’s immediate question.
- Ask only the questions needed to check fit.
- Offer a specific next step.

### After the first conversation

- Record the lead stage and reason for disqualification.
- Follow up with context instead of sending a generic reminder.
- Review patterns across campaigns, sources, and team members.

## Judge the system, not one isolated metric

Paid ads are responsible for creating relevant interest. The landing page, response time, customer service conversation, qualification rules, and trial-booking process determine how much of that interest becomes a real opportunity.

When lead quality looks weak, inspect the whole path before blaming one channel. The evidence may point to targeting or creative, but it may also reveal a follow-up problem that better ads cannot solve.
