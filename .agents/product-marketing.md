# Product Marketing Context

**Document version:** v1
**Last updated:** 2026-09-29

## Product Overview

**One-liner:** Radhi helps tutoring and language-course businesses turn paid ads into contactable prospects and qualified trial bookings by connecting the offer, campaign, tracking, and lead-response workflow.

**What it does:** Radhi builds and improves the journey from offer and paid campaign through lead capture, qualification, CS follow-up, and trial booking. The work treats lead generation and lead handling as one connected system instead of optimizing only clicks or form submissions.

**Product category:** Paid lead-generation and trial-booking systems for education businesses.

**Product type:** Productized professional service with strategy, implementation, measurement, and optimization.

**Business model:** Project or ongoing service. Pricing and packaging are not yet fixed. Radhi is in the first year of establishing the independent offer and may initially accept broader work, but public positioning should remain focused.

## Target Audience

**Target companies:** Initially, children's tutoring businesses and language-course providers. Potential future expansion includes test-preparation and professional-certification providers.

**Decision-makers:** Owners, founders, marketing leaders, and enrollment or admissions managers.

**Primary use case:** Improve paid lead generation when a business reports that its ads produce too many low-quality or unresponsive leads.

**Jobs to be done:**

- Clarify the offer and campaign message so prospects understand what is being promoted, including important price information when appropriate.
- Generate contactable prospects who match the education provider's intended audience and learning goals.
- Connect campaign tracking with CS follow-up and trial-booking outcomes.
- Identify whether weak results come from acquisition quality, delayed first contact, inconsistent follow-up, or another part of the lead flow.

**Use cases:**

- A tutoring business receives many form submissions, but CS says the leads are bad.
- A language course needs more qualified trial bookings from Google Ads or Meta Ads.
- Marketing cannot see what happens after a lead is handed to CS or admissions.
- New leads are contacted too slowly or without a consistent response workflow.

## Problems & Pain Points

**Core problem:** Education businesses often describe the problem as "our ads bring bad leads," but the actual failure may occur in the offer, price communication, targeting, first-response time, follow-up workflow, or feedback loop between marketing and admissions.

**Why alternatives fall short:**

- Platform-focused advertisers may stop at clicks, form submissions, or cost per lead.
- Marketing and CS may use different definitions of a good lead.
- A reachable prospect may be labeled bad after delayed or inconsistent follow-up.
- Hidden or unclear pricing can attract people whose expectations do not match the offer.
- Lead outcomes are often not returned to marketing, preventing meaningful optimization.

**What it costs them:** Wasted ad spend, lost prospects, unnecessary conflict between marketing and CS, misleading performance conclusions, and missed trial or enrollment opportunities.

**Emotional tension:** Owners feel they are paying for leads nobody wants. Marketing feels blamed for failures after the handoff. CS feels burdened by unresponsive contacts. Nobody has a shared view of where prospects are being lost.

## Competitive Landscape

**Direct:** Paid-ads freelancers and agencies serving education businesses. They may focus on campaign setup and platform metrics without connecting performance to CS handling or trial bookings.

**Secondary:** General digital-marketing agencies offering websites, content, SEO, and ads. Their breadth may not translate into ownership of the complete lead-to-trial workflow.

**Indirect:** In-house staff, referrals, organic social media, and education marketplace listings. These may generate demand but do not necessarily provide a measurable and controllable acquisition system.

## Differentiation

**Key differentiators:**

- Practical understanding of the education journey from offer to enrollment.
- Connects paid acquisition with lead handling instead of stopping at lead submission.
- Distinguishes a contactable lead, engaged prospect, qualified prospect, trial booking, trial attendance, and enrollment.
- Reviews price communication, response speed, follow-up workflow, and downstream outcomes when diagnosing lead quality.
- Can implement supporting components such as landing pages, websites, tracking, analytics, WhatsApp workflows, dashboards, and CS scripts when they are needed by the lead-generation system.

**How we do it differently:** Treat the campaign, tracking, qualification, and CS response process as one system. Diagnose whether the reported "bad lead" problem comes from acquisition or from what happens after the lead arrives.

**Why that's better:** The client receives a more truthful diagnosis, better feedback for campaign optimization, and fewer avoidable losses between form submission and trial booking.

**Why customers choose us:** They need a hands-on partner who can understand and connect strategy, advertising, technical setup, measurement, and operational follow-up.

## Objections

| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| "Our ads already bring bad leads." | First determine whether the problem is audience fit, offer and price communication, contactability, response time, or follow-up execution. |
| "Can you guarantee enrollment?" | No. The engagement can improve the system through trial booking and use enrollment data for feedback, but enrollment depends on factors outside the agreed scope. |
| "Why do you need access to lead outcomes?" | Without downstream outcomes, campaigns can optimize only for submissions rather than contactable prospects and trial bookings. |

**Anti-persona:** Businesses expecting guaranteed enrollment; businesses unwilling to share lead outcomes; and businesses without anyone available to contact new leads promptly. Lack of a large ad budget is not automatically disqualifying.

## Switching Dynamics

**Push:** The business is spending on ads but receives complaints about lead quality, cannot trace leads through the funnel, or loses prospects after submission.

**Pull:** A connected system that improves the offer, campaign, measurement, and CS workflow through trial booking.

**Habit:** Judging performance only by lead volume or cost per lead; labeling unresponsive contacts as bad leads; contacting leads days later; and allowing marketing and CS to work without shared definitions or data.

**Anxiety:** Fear that the ads will continue wasting money, that operational changes will create more work, or that better tracking will reveal problems outside marketing.

## Customer Language

**How they describe the problem:**

- "Our ads bring bad leads."
- "The leads don't reply."
- "We get leads, but they don't book a trial."

**How we describe the response:**

- "Let's determine whether they are genuinely bad leads or good prospects being lost through unclear pricing, delayed contact, or inconsistent follow-up."

**Words to use:** Qualified trial bookings, contactable prospects, lead quality, first-response time, follow-up workflow, offer, trial booking, admissions, CS, tracking, feedback loop.

**Words to avoid:** Guaranteed enrollment, effortless growth, unlimited leads, full-service digital marketing, and vague claims such as "optimize your online presence."

**Glossary:**

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Lead | A person who submits their contact information or starts an enquiry |
| Contactable lead | A lead with valid contact details who can reasonably be reached |
| Engaged prospect | A contactable lead who responds or meaningfully interacts with CS or admissions |
| Qualified prospect | An engaged prospect who matches agreed audience, demographic, and learning-goal criteria |
| Trial booked | A prospect who schedules a trial or equivalent consultation or assessment |
| Trial attended | A prospect who actually participates in the scheduled trial |
| Enrolled | A prospect who purchases or formally joins the course |

## Blog Editorial Foundation

### Reference and reading experience

- Use WordStream as the primary structural reference for practical, useful marketing education.
- Use Close as a secondary reference for clear explanations of sales and follow-up operations.
- Do not use Finalsite as a reference for this blog.
- Keep a consistent reading experience across articles, while allowing the format to fit the subject: practical diagnosis, how-to guide, or practitioner note.
- Do not force every article into a numbered list. Use headings, paragraphs, examples, and lists only where they make the explanation easier to follow.

### Default article approach

Most articles should help an education business operator understand and act on a real problem:

1. Name a recognizable problem or situation.
2. State the main viewpoint early so the reader knows what the article will help them understand.
3. Explain the reasoning from actual experience: what was observed, what was done, and what was learned. Use this only when the experience is real and safe to share.
4. Offer practical next steps and explain why each step may help, including relevant limits or conditions.
5. Help the reader evaluate their own situation or reflect on what evidence to collect.
6. End with one relevant next step or call to action when it fits the article.

Adapt this sequence when another structure better serves the topic. There is no mandatory word count, table of contents, FAQ, or video. Do not add these elements unless they help the reader.

### Evidence and honesty

- Distinguish clearly between personal experience, factual claims supported by a source, and illustrative examples created to explain an idea.
- Label illustrative examples as examples. Never present them as client cases, observed outcomes, or personal anecdotes.
- Never invent client results, metrics, quotes, customer stories, or personal experience.
- Qualify sourced claims accurately and link or cite the source where the publishing format allows it.
- Keep claims consistent with the current service positioning and responsibility boundary. Do not promise guaranteed leads, trial bookings, or enrollment.

### Lead-quality diagnosis

- Distinguish a lead who does not respond or cannot be reached from a lead who is unqualified. These are different outcomes and need different evidence.
- Do not reflexively blame customer service, admissions, advertising, or the prospect. Explain what to inspect before deciding where the cause lies.
- Consider the evidence across the offer and price message, audience fit, valid contact details, first-response time, response attempts, conversation quality, qualification criteria, and trial-booking handoff.
- Make clear when available evidence is insufficient to assign a cause. Recommend what to track or review next.
- Use the terms in the existing glossary consistently, especially contactable lead, engaged prospect, qualified prospect, and trial booked.

### Audience and language

- Write for owners, operators, marketing leads, and CS or admissions teams at tutoring and language-course businesses.
- Keep examples relevant to their day-to-day work, such as enquiries, class fit, course pricing, response workflows, trial sessions, and enrollment handoffs.
- Write Indonesian naturally for Indonesian business readers. Translate the meaning and tone rather than following English sentence structure word for word.
- Keep articles concise and practical. Remove repetition and advice that does not help the reader understand or act.

### Reusable article skeleton

Choose the headings that fit the subject. The bracketed prompts are editorial guidance and should not appear in a published article.

```markdown
# [Clear, specific article title]

[Opening: name the reader's situation and why it matters.]

[Main viewpoint: explain the key idea early.]

## [First useful question or part of the diagnosis]

[Explain what to examine and why. Separate facts, experience, and labeled examples.]

## [Next question, cause, or action]

[Give practical steps or explanation. Include limits and what the evidence can or cannot show.]

## [How to evaluate the situation]

[Help the reader review their own process, define a measure, or decide what to check next.]

[Relevant internal link or one contextual call to action, when useful.]
```
